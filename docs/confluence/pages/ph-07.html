PH07-S01 · React Native shell and a typed, versioned native bridge

Positive

  • React Native owns cross-platform navigation and the bridge contracts only; Swift and SwiftUI own the iOS and iPadOS screens, persistence, widgets, Keychain and notifications.

  • The bridge schema is versioned and tested on both sides, with a typed payload and a typed error for every message.

  • Deep links route through the shell to the native screen that opens the durable record.

  • The app supports and is tested on the owner's own iPhone and iPad, at the iOS they run, with the models the owner gives. MP40-Q12a, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Your own devices only" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options"). The option read: "Support and test the iPhone and iPad you own, at the iOS they run. You give the models."

  • The app lays out by window width, so a narrow iPad window uses the phone layout. MP40-Q12b, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Any window width" (pressed on the page). The option read: "The Apple app lays out by window width, so a narrow iPad window uses the phone layout."

Negative

  • A message with an unknown version or an invalid payload is refused with a typed error, never coerced.

  • No owner data crosses the bridge into logs.

  • The React Native shell does not imply an Android product screen.

Recovery

  • After an update with an incompatible bridge version, the app shows an update-required screen and keeps its cached read views.

PH07-S02 · Native SwiftUI journeys over the shared API

Positive

  • The approved journeys run natively on iPhone and iPad from a Swift client generated from FastAPI's OpenAPI schema.

  • SSE notices make the app re-read durable state; it never trusts a notice's content as data.

  • Every error shows its code with a recovery action, and carries the request_id and trace_id.

Negative

  • No financial logic exists only in the app.

  • A local Face ID unlock or an open app is not server authorisation: approvals still need the server's step-up (ARC-07).

  • No screen hides staleness or a provider's status.

Recovery

  • A lost connection keeps the last read with its age and a retry; an expired session returns to sign-in and then to the same screen.

PH07-S03 · Free seven-day local builds and the Apple test matrix

Positive

  • The app is signed with a free personal team and installed on the owner's iPhone and iPad from the owner's Mac; each install records its expiry seven days later.

  • Each pull request that touches the Apple app builds it and runs XCTest on a free macOS runner; the Maestro CLI journeys run on simulators on the owner's Mac, and real-device builds are made there. MP203-11, 24 November 2026 (MP-203): the owner picked "Build and unit tests in CI" (pressed on the page). The option read: "Each pull request that touches the Apple app builds it and runs XCTest on a free macOS runner. Maestro journeys and real-device builds stay on your Mac." Tests cover the owner's own iPhone and iPad at the iOS they run, including a narrow iPad window that uses the phone layout. MP40-Q12a, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Your own devices only" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options").

  • Sign-in in the app uses Cognito with Google through the system browser with PKCE, and tokens are kept in the Keychain.

Negative

  • No TestFlight, Apple push, Sign in with Apple or on-device passkeys is used until the Developer Program is bought (owner, 4 November 2026: "The purchase (Recommended)", MP-179). The bar covers the Apple app only; a phone's browser may enrol a passkey once a test proves it works. ADM-04, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Allowed in the browser" (pressed on the page).

  • No build is distributed to anyone else, and CI does not sign Apple builds.

  • A pull request whose Apple build or XCTest run fails on the macOS runner does not pass its checks.

Recovery

  • The app warns before its build expires; after expiry, reinstalling from the owner's Mac restores it, with the server as the source of truth.

PH07-S04 · Encrypted offline read cache with the age of every record

Positive

  • Only the five data kinds the offline screens show (holdings, prices, forecasts, notices and cost) are cached, each with its age, encrypted with iOS Data Protection and a Keychain-held key; a browser keeps the same five. MP40-Q12c, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Both keep the five" (pressed on the page).

  • Every cached record shows its age, and the app refetches on reconnect.

  • Sign-out, session revocation and an account change clear the cache.

Negative

  • No financial write (an approval, an import, a settings change or a confirm) is accepted offline; it is refused with its reason.

  • A cached record is never shown as fresh. A revoked device's copy clears on its next connection, and the app also stops showing its copy after a set time without reaching the server; the owner chooses that time, and it is not set yet. OWN-37, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Saved copy also times out" (pressed on the page). The option read: "The copy clears on the next connection, and the app also stops showing it after a set time without reaching the server. You choose the time." A browser's copy follows the same rule and the same time-out as the Apple app: it clears on its next connection and stops showing after the same time, one value the owner still sets. MP203-01, 24 November 2026 (MP-203): the owner picked "Same rule, same time" (pressed on the page). The option read: "The browser's copy clears on its next connection and stops showing after the same time-out as the Apple app. One value, which you still set." How well an offline app or a browser can enforce this is unverified.

Recovery

  • On reconnect, stale records are replaced; a corrupt cache is discarded and rebuilt from the server.

PH07-S05 · No confidential holdings on the lock screen, app snapshots, widgets or shared content

Positive

  • Notification text is neutral, the app switcher snapshot is covered, and widgets show no holdings by default.

  • Shared or exported content leaves out holdings values unless the owner has approved a setting that allows them (PH05-S14).

Negative

  • No value or holding name appears on the lock screen by default.

  • A share sheet never attaches a private export without the owner's explicit action.

Recovery

  • If a privacy setting change fails, the private default stays in force.

PH07-S06 · Native accessibility: VoiceOver, Dynamic Type, reduced motion and chart alternatives

Positive

  • Every control and chart has a VoiceOver label, and every chart has a text summary of its range and uncertainty.

  • Dynamic Type works up to the largest sizes without truncating risk or freshness text, and iPad layouts adapt.

  • Reduced motion removes animation, and focus order follows reading order.

Negative

  • No information is carried by colour alone.

  • No animation plays when reduced motion is on.

Recovery

  • A layout that cannot fit at a text size falls back to a single scrolling column rather than clipping text.

PH07-S07 · Phone notifications through Web Push from the PWA

Positive

  • The PWA asks for notification permission only after an owner action and stores one push subscription per device, owner and environment.

  • Notices leave from a durable outbox with a dedup key; each attempt and the push service's acceptance are recorded as delivery attempts, not as read.

  • Payloads are neutral; a tap opens a deep link that re-reads durable state after sign-in.

  • Quiet hours (22:00 to 07:00 Europe/London) apply, and only a critical fault on the named list in PH06-S12 and PH08-S05 pushes during them; no investment alert does (MP203-02 and MP203-03, recorded in PH06-S12). Held notices arrive with the 07:30 brief on weekdays; with no brief on Saturday or Sunday, held alerts land in the in-app inbox at 07:30 (proposed). MP40-Q11b, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Weekdays only" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options").

  • Web Push support on the owner's iPhone and iPad (as a home-screen web app) and browsers is verified before it is relied on.

Negative

  • Denied or background-disabled permission never hides an alert: it stays in the in-app inbox (PH07-S08).

  • MP never promises instant delivery and never infers no risk from silence.

  • No Apple push (APNs) path is built until the owner joins the Developer Program.

Recovery

  • An expired or rejected subscription is marked dead and the owner is asked to re-enable it; retries reuse the dedup key, so the owner sees one notice.

PH07-S08 · In-app notice inbox on web and Apple, the fallback for every alert

Positive

  • Every notification intent appears in the in-app inbox on the web and the Apple app, ordered by detection time, with read state synced across devices.

  • Each entry deep-links to its evidence.

Negative

  • The inbox never omits an alert because a push succeeded or failed.

  • Opening a notice never performs an action.

Recovery

  • On reconnect the inbox is rebuilt from the server; a read-state conflict resolves to read.

PH07-S09 · Native capability decision table: launch or later, with nothing dropped silently

Positive

  • The table lists widgets, share-sheet statement intake, Spotlight search and deep links, Siri and App Intents, Live Activities for long jobs, Apple push, Sign in with Apple, on-device passkeys and TestFlight, each with its privacy, availability, cost and fallback.

  • Capabilities that need the paid Apple Developer Program are marked later until the Developer Program is bought (MP-179), with today's fallback (Web Push, Google sign-in, local builds).

  • Each row becomes launch-required or later only by the owner's explicit choice, recorded with its date and words. The owner's choice is recorded: widgets and statement sharing come at launch, and Spotlight, Siri and Live Activities are recorded as later. MP40-Q13, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Widgets and share first" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options"). The option read: "Widgets and statement sharing come at launch, and Spotlight, Siri and Live Activities are recorded as later." Whether widgets and sharing work on free seven-day builds is unverified; the option's cost note said that if either does not, it moves to later.

  • This story also builds the two capabilities picked for launch: widgets and share-sheet statement intake, each with its privacy kept by PH07-S05 (MP-166). MP203-07, 24 November 2026 (MP-203): the owner picked "Widen three existing stories" (pressed on the page). The option read: "MP-47 gains the unlock step, MP-107 the factsheet reader and MP-163 the two builds." Either moves to later if it does not work on free seven-day builds, as the 5 November option's cost note said.

  • A statement shared to MP from another app enters the validated upload intake (PH01-S06) as a new source, for the owner's review like any upload, and a widget shows the age of its data and opens the app by a deep link (proposed).

Negative

  • No capability is removed without an owner decision record.

  • Android product screens stay outside the agreed release scope.

  • A widget shows no holdings unless the owner turns them on, and a shared statement is never parsed, approved or posted by sharing alone (proposed).

Recovery

  • A changed owner choice creates a new table version; the previous version is kept.

  • A share that fails or is cancelled imports nothing and says so, and a widget that cannot reach fresh data shows its age, never a fresh-looking figure (proposed).
