PH08-S01 · Production releases that wait for the owner's approval

Positive

  • Each commit on main builds one artefact once; GitHub OIDC deploys it to dev. A merge to main starts the release, which waits in the GitHub production environment for the owner's approval before it promotes the artefact to prod. MP203-08, 24 November 2026 (MP-203): the owner picked "An environment replaces route A" (pressed on the page). The option read: "A merge to main starts the release, which waits in the production environment for your approval." It replaces route A, the release workflow only the owner could start (GitHub Free; owner, 3 November 2026; PL-11). Administrators can bypass environment rules by default (recorded 24 November 2026).

  • Each release writes a ReleaseEvidence record: the commit, the artefact hash, its SBOM, the gate results, the Terraform plan, the owner's approval in the production environment and the previous release to roll back to.

  • Prod Terraform applies run only through this route from main, after the owner approves the reviewed plan.

  • Rollback switches the Lambda alias to the previous release's version.

Negative

  • A Jira status, a draft approval or a green dev deploy never proves release readiness and never approves a prod release.

  • No laptop or Claude session applies to prod, and no actor other than the owner can approve a release waiting in the production environment.

  • No release proceeds with a failing gate, an unreviewed plan or drift.

  • The route rests on the repository staying public and on the administrator bypass staying unused, as the option's cost note said; a release that used the bypass is flagged in its ReleaseEvidence, and if the repository goes private, releases stop until the owner chooses a route again (proposed).

Recovery

  • A failed prod deploy rolls back by alias to the previous release; a failed apply stops and the next attempt starts from a fresh reviewed plan; drift blocks the release until it is resolved through Terraform.

PH08-S02 · Onboard real holdings, listings and statements once each is checked

Positive

  • A named live universe lists the owner's holdings, the curated list of about 150 discovery names and the top-5 segments, each listing with its entitlement, source and coverage verified (UK funds by the Grow 55 first-month test, else statements; Indian funds from the owner's statements).

  • Each real statement layout is checked against the named layout coverage before its first real import; unsupported layouts go to review or manual entry.

  • Additions follow the owner's rule of 3 November 2026: Claude proposes, code checks coverage and credit cost with the provider, and the owner confirms in the app.

  • A remaining-gap decision lists what is not covered, and the owner signs it.

Negative

  • No live claim, accuracy figure or training result comes from synthetic fixtures.

  • Real data never enters dev, and no deployment or spending follows from onboarding.

Recovery

  • A failed real import is reversed through the import reversal path, and an unverified listing stays watch-only or unavailable with its reason.

PH08-S03 · Monthly restore exercise into an isolated target

Positive

  • Recovery point and recovery time objectives are agreed and recorded per record class: ledger, audit, approvals, originals, forecasts and outcomes, model pointers.

  • Every month, and after any storage, import or audit change, those records are restored into an isolated recovery target and reconciled by counts, hashes and financial balances. An exercise is overdue when a month ends with none, or a change has none by the next release. ADM-24, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Tie to month and release" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options"). The option read: "Overdue means a month ended with no exercise, or a change has none by the next release."

  • Each exercise writes a RecoveryExercise record with its result.

Negative

  • No destructive restore into production; the recovery target takes no authority until reconciliation passes and the owner approves a switch.

  • A restore reapplies deletion tombstones, so deleted content is never brought back.

Recovery

  • A failed exercise opens an incident and blocks the next release until it is fixed and re-run.

PH08-S04 · Rollback exercises for the app, contracts, models and imports

Positive

  • On dev, rehearse: an app rollback by Lambda alias, a contract rollback where the old reader still works, a model and calibration revert to the previous approved model with the owner notified, and a failed import reversal.

  • The automatic model revert rules (owner, 3 November 2026: 4 bad weeks running, or 2 during the 4-week probation) are exercised with synthetic scores.

  • Each rehearsal writes a RecoveryExercise record. A rehearsal is overdue when a month ends with none, or a change has none by the next release. ADM-24, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Tie to month and release" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options").

Negative

  • A rollback never deletes a forecast, an outcome or an audit record, and switching forward again needs the owner's yes.

  • No rollback is rehearsed in prod.

Recovery

  • A failed rehearsal opens an incident and blocks the next release until it passes.

PH08-S05 · Incidents, alert classes and evidence correlation

Positive

  • Alert classes stay separate: financial risk, system security, data quality, billing and job failure (CT-18), each with deduplication and correlation.

  • Each incident links alert, run, source and model evidence by id.

  • Alerts exist for a missed forecast run, a stalled collector, stale billing, dead-letter queue depth, expired source access and out-of-order conversation events.

  • Phone notifications go through the web app's Web Push until the owner buys the Apple Developer Program (owner, 3 November 2026).

  • Between 22:00 and 07:00 Europe/London only a critical fault pushes, and critical means this named list, each with its alert: a sign-in, passkey or recovery the owner did not start; a device revoke that fails; production lost (ADM-05); and a failed backup or ledger write. MP203-03, 24 November 2026 (MP-203): the owner picked "A short named list" (on a question card in chat). The option read: "Proposed: a sign-in, passkey or recovery you did not start; a device revoke that fails; production lost (ADM-05); and a failed backup or ledger write. Everything else, such as a missed run or a stalled collector, waits for 07:30." Every other alert, such as a missed run or a stalled collector, waits for 07:30.

  • When production stops answering its checks, code opens the incident and the owner records production lost with a fresh sign-in. ADM-05, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Code flags, you confirm" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options").

Negative

  • A health endpoint that reports OK while a dead-letter queue holds items or a daily run was missed fails its test.

  • Alerts and incidents carry no holdings or secrets.

  • No alert outside the named critical list pushes during quiet hours.

Recovery

  • An incident closes only with a recorded resolution and reopens if the fault recurs.

PH08-S06 · Bounded replay and self-recovery

Positive

  • An allowlist of automatic recovery actions: retry an idempotent job within its bounds, replay a dead-letter item up to a limit, re-run a missed collection, resume a durable workflow. A late price is not a missed collection: it stays a data quality incident, and a newer price arrives at the next check. ADM-06, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "No, keep it apart" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options").

  • A ReplayRequest records scope, reason, items, idempotency keys and, where needed, the owner's approval.

  • Failure injection covers queue and dead-letter limits, out-of-order conversation events, a market outage, a missed forecast, expired source access and a stalled collector; each recovers or escalates as designed.

Negative

  • No agent edits infrastructure, buys a provider, raises spending or deletes evidence.

  • Broad replay, financial reprocessing and new paid external calls need the owner's approval with step-up.

  • A replay never crosses environments.

Recovery

  • A replay that keeps failing stops at its bound and escalates to an incident.

PH08-S07 · Whole-release gate report: security, science, agents, accessibility and privacy

Positive

  • The report runs API and schema contract tests, ledger correctness, scientific checks (chronological validation, no leakage), agent evaluations, accessibility at the standard the owner approves, native and offline tests, and the privacy and security scans (OSV-Scanner, pip-audit, gitleaks, Checkov).

  • Coverage is shown per market and per horizon, with gaps marked unavailable.

  • Blockers, non-blocking issues and rollback readiness are listed; any independent review is offered to the owner separately.

Negative

  • A missing or failed gate is never green: the report fails closed.

  • The live model gate (the 8-week shadow trial and owner promotion) stays separate from application release approval.

Recovery

  • A failed gate blocks the release; the report is re-run after the fix and earlier reports are kept.

PH08-S08 · Prove the full launch workload fits the £100 month

Positive

  • The full launch workload is measured on dev, including the end-of-day scan of about 150 names (COST-Q3), and the month is recomputed across dev and prod AWS, data, Claude, evaluations, collectors, SSE, backups and builds.

  • Unverified-credit and no-credit cases show the degraded modes and a month within £100.

  • The cost model gains the measured lines not yet in it: Terraform state, internet data transfer out and the scan's compute and storage.

Negative

  • A stress result never authorises spending, and no paid call is made for the proof without the owner's yes.

Recovery

  • If the measured month passes the £60 warning, the result and options go to the owner; nothing is cut silently.

Recovery
