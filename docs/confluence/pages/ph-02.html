PH02-S01 · Owner sign-in with Google and passkeys through Cognito, linked only on proof

Positive

  • Terraform in infra/terraform/, plan-only until the owner asks for an apply in the running prompt, defines one Cognito user pool per account (dev and prod) in us-east-1 on a tier that supports passkeys (Essentials or Plus), with Google as a provider and passkeys enabled.

  • Linking a Google identity to the owner uses the provider's stable subject after a fresh sign-in with both identities.

  • The API accepts only tokens from its own environment's pool and app client: a dev token is refused by prod, a prod token by dev, and any other user's token everywhere.

  • Sign-in and link events are written to the audit with opaque references only.

  • A passkey may be enrolled in a phone's browser once a test proves that Cognito passkeys work there without Apple's paid programme; until that test (MP-197) passes, phone web offers no enrolment. ADM-04, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Allowed in the browser" (pressed on the page). The option read: "The bar covers the Apple app only, so Phone web may enrol a passkey once a test proves it works."

Negative

  • Email equality alone never links or merges identities.

  • Sign in with Apple is not configured until the owner buys the Apple Developer Program (owner, 3 November 2026).

  • No identity token, refresh token or authorisation code appears in a log, trace, audit record or error message.

  • Repeated failed sign-ins are rate-limited without revealing whether an account exists.

Recovery

  • If Google is unavailable, passkey sign-in still works; a link that fails midway leaves both identities unlinked and the attempt audited.

PH02-S02 · Owner recovery paths, lost-device recovery and session revocation

Positive

  • A recovery inventory (CT-17) holds at least two independent recovery paths, for example passkeys on two separate devices plus the verified Google link, and is shown to the owner.

  • Revoking a session or device ends its refresh tokens, closes its live streams and removes any passkey held on that device, so the path count drops by one, within the revocation time the owner approves (CT-17). ADM-02, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Revoke removes it too" (pressed on the page). The option read: "Revoking a device also removes any passkey held on it, and your path count drops by one." Whether Cognito can remove a passkey this way, and whether a passkey synced across the owner's Apple devices ends on those too, are unverified until MP-197 tests them. The revocation time is set from measured proof before the identity phase ends and is not set yet. OWN-39, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Anything that writes or widens" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options").

  • Lost device: from any healthy path, passkey or Google, the owner signs in fresh, revokes the lost device and enrols a replacement passkey; each step is audited. ADM-03, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Any healthy path" (pressed on the page). The option read: "A fresh sign-in on any healthy path, passkey or Google, can revoke, and Privacy's button changes to match." With the Mac lost, the replacement passkey is enrolled in the phone's browser if browser passkeys are allowed; the iPhone app cannot do this step until the Developer Program is bought. ADM-15, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Finish in the phone browser" (pressed on the page). The option read: "You enrol the replacement passkey in the phone's browser, if you allow browser passkeys." The browser step rests on the MP-197 test; if it fails, recovery ends on one path (proposed).

  • A loss-of-all-devices procedure is written and rehearsed on dev, recovering through the remaining provider path.

Negative

  • Removing or unlinking the last usable sign-in path is refused.

  • Recovery never relies on email equality, an SMS code or a support route that bypasses the owner's proof.

Recovery

  • A recovery interrupted midway leaves the old paths intact and the new one unconfirmed; it is resumed or cancelled, never half-applied.

PH02-S03 · Step-up for sensitive actions, bound to the exact change approved

Positive

  • Every act that changes a record, widens what MP may do or opens an original needs a fresh sign-in; reading and stopping do not. OWN-39, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Anything that writes or widens" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options"). The option read: "Every act that changes a record, widens what MP may do or opens an original needs a fresh sign-in; reading and stopping do not. Both times are set from measured proof before the identity phase ends." The sensitive-action matrix (CT-01) lists each such act, including import confirmation and corrections, deletion, replay, identity linking and recovery, budget expansion and model promotion, with its required assurance and freshness; the freshness time and the revocation time are not set yet.

  • The backend checks Cognito authentication strength and freshness per action and asks for step-up when the evidence is not enough (ARC-07); if Cognito cannot give that assurance, a verified fresh sign-in path meets the same policy.

  • An approval binds owner, environment, action, payload sha256, expected record or configuration version and expiry; a financial approval allows 15 minutes to start the exact commit (POLICY-001). Other proposals keep their own wait by kind; a replay's wait is not yet agreed. OWN-08, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "By kind, as drawn" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options"). The option read: "Each kind keeps its own wait: days for a promotion or a setting, none for a restart."

  • At execution, including after a durable wait, the backend rechecks authority, expiry and the expected version.

Negative

  • A local Face ID unlock, a refreshed token or an open app alone is refused as step-up.

  • A changed payload, an expired approval or a moved record version is refused and needs a fresh approval.

  • Workflow and audit state hold opaque evidence references, never live tokens.

Recovery

  • If execution fails after approval, a retry with the same payload and version inside the window is idempotent; the approval is never reused for a different payload, and after expiry the owner approves again.

PH02-S04 · Terraform foundation: organisation, dev and prod accounts, guard rails and state

Positive

  • Terraform in infra/terraform/, plan-only until the owner asks for an apply in the running prompt, defines AWS Organizations with all features, a management account that runs no workloads, and dev and prod member accounts in us-east-1 (ARC-11, MP-29).

  • The cost-guard SCP denies NAT gateways, interface endpoints, VPNs, load balancers, SageMaker endpoints and GPU instances until an ADR lifts it. Beside it, three further SCPs keep CloudTrail on, deny root actions and forbid leaving the organisation, binding the dev and prod accounts. MP40-Q4, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Add all three" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options"). The option read: "The three policies join the cost guard in Terraform, and they bind the dev and prod accounts."

  • State lives in a versioned, encrypted S3 bucket per account with S3 native locking, made by a bootstrap configuration that starts on local state and migrates into the bucket it creates.

  • GitHub OIDC deploy roles, one per account; the prod role trusts only the release that waits in the GitHub production environment for the owner's approval (PH08-S01, MP203-08). Proposed: it trusts only that environment, checked against GitHub's documentation before build.

  • Plan-only CI on every PR: terraform fmt -check, terraform validate, TFLint and Checkov, with no AWS access; the Terraform CLI and AWS provider are pinned in the lock file.

  • Every resource carries an environment tag and cost-allocation tags, with no em dashes in names, tags or descriptions.

Negative

  • No terraform apply runs unless the owner asks in the running prompt; prod is never applied from a laptop or a Claude session.

  • No Region allowlist SCP and no resource control policies (owner, 3 November 2026).

  • No dev role trusts a prod role, or the reverse.

Recovery

  • A failed bootstrap is re-run idempotently from local state; local state is removed only after a plan against the migrated state shows no changes.

PH02-S05 · Private encrypted storage and least-privilege roles in each environment

Positive

  • Terraform in infra/terraform/, plan-only until the owner asks for an apply in the running prompt, defines per account: a customer KMS key; private S3 buckets with Block Public Access and versioning and the prefixes sources/uploads/, sources/provider/, evidence/, training/, artefacts/candidates/ and artefacts/approved/; DynamoDB tables for the ledger, market data, forecasts, audit, jobs and costs; and SSM SecureString parameters created with a placeholder and ignore_changes on the value.

  • Each role (API, ledger writer, data workers, one role per Claude route, inference, training orchestrator, training job, billing collector) has exactly the access in the region-network boundary matrix and no more.

  • Prod tables, buckets, user pools and keys have prevent_destroy and each service's own deletion protection.

  • IAM Access Analyzer reports no external access.

Negative

  • No dev principal appears in any prod policy; a dev role acting on a prod resource is denied, so synthetic dev data cannot reach prod.

  • Only the worker that needs a secret can read it (the Twelve Data key for data workers, each route's own Anthropic key), and Terraform state never holds a real secret value.

  • Only the ledger writer can write the ledger; the API, agents and other workers cannot.

Recovery

  • Versioning and deletion protection make an accidental overwrite or delete recoverable, and a plan that would replace a protected resource fails.

PH02-S06 · Durable job records, an outbox and idempotent retries

Positive

  • Every job (import, daily forecast, collection, Claude route call, training submission) has a JobRecord with a stable business id, an idempotency key, attempts, a lease, and accepted, started and terminal evidence that survives a crash (CT-05).

  • A commit writes its business records, audit entry and outbox entry in one conditional DynamoDB transaction, and consumers deduplicate by consumer_dedup_key.

  • Uploaded bytes and database pointers are not one transaction: the S3 object is written and hashed first, and its pointer is published only after its manifest verifies.

  • Retries use bounded exponential backoff; past the bound the item goes to its dead-letter queue and quarantine with a reason.

  • Lambda Durable Functions steps are named and versioned, replay is deterministic, and an ambiguous external call (a paid call or a started training job) is reconciled rather than repeated (ARC-03).

Negative

  • A timeout or replay never produces a second ledger commit, a second provider charge or a second training job for the same idempotency key.

  • A dev job cannot be replayed into prod.

  • A job record never holds document contents, tokens or prompts, only references.

Recovery

  • After a crash a job resumes from its last durable step or is marked reconcile_required, and a job past the stuck-run threshold the owner approves (CT-05) raises an incident.

PH02-S07 · Billing collectors for AWS daily and Anthropic every six hours

Positive

  • The prod billing collector assumes the read-only Cost Explorer role in the management account (owner, 3 November 2026) once a day, covers both accounts, and stays within the 120-page monthly cap.

  • Anthropic usage and cost are collected every six hours through an Anthropic admin key that only the prod collector reads, from secure storage. MP40-Q5, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Adopt the key" (pressed on the page). The option read: "The prod collector alone reads it from secure storage every six hours and fills the Claude line. Ready stands." The key's price and whether the account can have one are unverified, and any charge counts against the £100 ceiling; until a key exists, that collection shows as unavailable.

  • Each collection stores the source time, the collected time and estimate or reconciled status, and upserts idempotently by period.

  • The dashboard reads cached aggregates only: refreshing it 100 times makes no billing API call (COST-AT-08).

Negative

  • A usage report is never treated as a credit balance (COST-AT-09).

  • The collector has no data-plane access and writes only the cost table.

  • Pages beyond the cap are deferred and the collection is marked partial, never silently cut short.

Recovery

  • A failed run is retried at the next window; a gap is backfilled once the source returns, and a collection older than its window is shown stale.

PH02-S08 · Cost meter on the main dashboard

Positive

  • The meter shows dev, prod, local API use and shared unallocated costs, with drill-down by provider, agent, model, feature and service, and each figure's source age.

  • Live estimates are kept apart from delayed billing, and gross usage apart from credit applied and from cash.

  • The month's encumbered cash is shown against £60, £75, £85 and the £100 ceiling.

  • Collector failures and reconciliation differences are shown.

Negative

  • Unknown shared credit stays unknown and is never multiplied per environment or per route.

  • The meter never shows a credit balance it cannot prove.

Recovery

Not applicable: The meter writes nothing; it reads cached aggregates from PH02-S07 and shows their stale, partial or failed states.

PH02-S09 · Atomic cost reservations before any paid work

Positive

  • A CostReservation (costs/reservation-contract.json) is written before any paid external work, scoped by owner, month, environment, route and job, with an idempotency key and a price snapshot.

  • Encumbered cash is settled cash plus outstanding unreconciled liability plus future fixed commitments plus open reservations, and work is admitted only when its worst-case cash fits the global, environment and route remainders.

  • When two workers reserve the final £0.05, at most one succeeds (COST-AT-06).

  • Reservations move only through reserved, dispatched, ambiguous, settled, cancel_pending and released as the contract defines, and settlement replaces the reservation, never adds to it.

Negative

  • With no verified credit allocation, optional Claude calls are refused whatever the cash headroom until the owner gives the explicit word that starts paid use; admissible credit is zero today (COST-AT-05). OWN-04, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Approve paid Claude at launch" (pressed on the page). The option read: "You approve paid use, so chat, research and explanations can run from day one, each route after passing its evaluation." Picking it does not turn paid calls on: they still need the owner's explicit word when the time comes.

  • No paid fallback switches on by itself.

Recovery

  • A batch that times out after the provider accepted it keeps its liability until reconciled and is not resubmitted (COST-AT-07); an expired reservation releases only confirmed unspent liability.

The option read: "Code cuts backfills first, then challenger models, then Claude jobs, and says which step it reached." The option read: "At £85 optional paid work stops and daily forecasts carry on. Cost paused shows only where a forecast needs that paid work."

  • The top-5 scan spends at most £5 a month, a reservation that would cross that cap is refused (COST-AT-18), and a scan overrun stops at the Claude cap.

  • In an Apple fee month, if the owner ever buys the Developer Program, optional work is admitted on the month without the fee (COST-Q4) and the full-month flags stay recorded.

  • Sign-in, ledger reads, audit, recovery and daily forecasts stay available at every threshold, inside the £15 kept above the £85 pause. MP40-Q6, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Approve all three" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options"). The option read: "Past 36 hours relevant optional work waits. Credit counts only with proof under 30 minutes old. The £15 above the £85 pause stays for sign-in, ledger reads, audit and recovery." Asked whether daily forecasts run inside that £15, the owner said "Yes, add them" (5 November 2026).

  • AWS billing older than 36 hours blocks the relevant optional admission until reconciled, and credit counts only with proof under 30 minutes old (COST-07, CT-13). MP40-Q6, 5 November 2026 (MP-184): the owner picked "Approve all three" (by the owner's words in chat, "for the remaining pick up recommended options").

Negative

  • The thresholds are never described as an instant AWS shut-off; hard caps on requests, concurrency, timeouts and retention back them.

  • No feature is cut silently: every degraded mode is shown to the owner with its reason.

Recovery

  • When cash falls back under a threshold (after reconciliation or at a new month), optional work is admitted again at the next check, and a paused job resumes from its durable step.

PH02-S11 · Authenticated, versioned API on Lambda

Positive

  • FastAPI runs on ordinary Lambda through the Lambda Web Adapter (buffered) behind API Gateway under /api/v1, defined in Terraform in infra/terraform/, plan-only until the owner asks for an apply in the running prompt.

  • Every route except health needs a valid token from its own environment's Cognito pool, and responses are Pydantic schemas, never storage records.

  • Every error carries a code, a message, the request_id and the trace_id, and nothing secret or private.

  • The web app uses cookie sessions with CSRF protection; the Apple app uses PKCE with tokens in the Keychain.

  • Sign-in-adjacent and expensive routes are rate-limited.

Negative

  • An unauthenticated or other-environment request gets 401 or 403 and no data.

  • A contract test fails any route that returns a DynamoDB item directly.

  • The API cannot write the ledger; it only enqueues work for the ledger writer.

Recovery

  • Commands are idempotent by key: a retry after a timeout returns the original result, not a second effect.

PH02-S12 · Authenticated, resumable server-sent events

Positive

  • A streaming proof comes first: API Gateway REST response streaming through the Lambda Web Adapter, alongside the ADOT layer, works on dev in us-east-1; until it passes, clients poll at the cadence the owner approves (PL-04).

  • Events carry only an event id, a type, a time and a resource reference, with heartbeats; streams are short-lived, inside API Gateway's 15-minute limit and 5-minute idle timeout.

  • A reconnect with Last-Event-ID resumes from the cursor while it is retained; after a gap the client re-reads the durable records.

  • Connections are capped per device and per tab, and a token expiry or revocation closes the stream.

  • Connection cost is measured on dev and fits the AWS allocation (one stream held for a month is about $21.60).

Negative

  • No holdings, credentials, provider payloads, model artefacts or financial truth travel in a stream.

  • The starter's unauthenticated sample stream is not production evidence and is removed or put behind authentication.

  • An expired or revoked session cannot keep a stream open.

Recovery

  • Duplicate or out-of-order events are dropped by id, and a missed range triggers a full re-read.

PH02-S13 · Redacted operational telemetry with linked traces

Positive

  • Logs are kept 14 days, rich traces 30 days and run summaries 90 days, inside the measured telemetry budget (about $4.94 a month).

  • OpenTelemetry through the ADOT layer to X-Ray, sampled: one trace per request the owner waits on, while queued, resumed, scheduled and batched work starts a new trace joined by business ids and span links (PL-12).

  • Correlation ids point to access-controlled evidence, not its content.

  • A small set of alarms covers a missed daily run, a stale collector, a non-empty dead-letter queue and the error rate.

Negative

  • Redaction tests prove that document text, holdings, account numbers, tokens and prompts never appear in logs or spans.

  • Telemetry is never the only record of a forecast, an approval or a ledger change.

Recovery

  • When a span is missing or sampled out, the Activity view stays complete from the durable records.

PH02-S14 · Record-class retention and the private archive of original files

Positive

  • A record-class table: core ledger history, approvals, business audit, model promotions and forecast and outcome evidence for the life of the account (POLICY-010); originals 24 months, renewable; logs 14 days, traces 30, run summaries 90.

  • Originals are encrypted in private versioned S3, with derivatives and backups classed separately.

  • Financial, forecast, approval and source audit records each keep their own durable lifecycle.

  • The owner can extend an original's retention or delete it with step-up; deletion writes a DeletionTombstone and reaches derivatives and caches.

Negative

  • Nothing expires automatically yet: reaching 24 months raises a renewal prompt, never a delete.

  • A deleted source is shown as unavailable evidence where a permitted record keeps a reference to it, and is never silently brought back.

Recovery

  • A restore reapplies tombstones before any data is made available (CT-04).
