Prism

Every market segment is cut from its own colour. Each field is a deep jewel split by hard facets of its own hue, like light through glass, so white words on it stay sharp at 20% screen brightness. Everything around it stays cold and exact: a grouped daylight ground and white cards in light, violet-black ink in dark, one near-black ink, every edge drawn. Colour is the field; ink is the word.

Hybrid A · Fan shares this UX system: the same type, shapes, components, states, patterns, motion and Claude. Only the identity differs.

Tap a field: the hero card takes its colour and its cut, and its figure rolls. Tap it again to return to the portfolio.

Principles

Six rules decide every screen. Each one says what it rules out, so a builder can tell when a design breaks it.

Foundations

One token sheet for the web and the Apple apps: MyPaisa's type and motion on a cold ground, with every edge drawn. Every value below is read live from this page's CSS, both themes at once.

Colour: ground, surfaces and ink

Cold daylight in light: a grouped ground under white cards, pure white for large surfaces, one near-black ink. Violet-black ink in dark: surfaces one step lighter, pure white words. The neutrals stay cold, so the segment fields are the only heat on the screen. At 20% brightness the best any pair can do is 5:1, so primary text sits at the extremes.

Colour: meaning, action and forecast

One action colour: a dark ink fill in light and near white in dark, because a filled button's label must reach 4.5:1 at 20%. Forecasts are violet: a median line over two bands. Scored outcomes are lagoon teal, scenarios plum and dotted. Claude's mark is magenta, a colour no figure uses. Gains are green with the rising mark; a routine fall is quiet ink with the falling mark; crimson is kept for alerts that ask for action. Every coloured word reaches 7:1, so it runs deep in light and pastel in dark.

The segment fields

Eight fields, one per segment, the same in both themes so a segment is recognised anywhere. US segments are cobalt, ultraviolet and azure; India's are saffron, orchid and fuchsia; the UK's is lagoon; metals are gold. Each is a deep jewel cut by three hard facets of its own hue, and each segment has its own cut, like a fingerprint. Words sit only on the two deep planes, where white labels hold the body tier (4.5:1 at 20%); the two bright facets stay in the top corner, clear of every word. The brightest facet also draws the hero's prism bar. A field never means gain or loss.

Type

Figures in SF Pro Rounded (Nunito on the web) at 800, tabular, tracked −0.03em. Everything else in SF Pro (Inter on the web) on Apple's Dynamic Type Large scale. Model ids and hashes in SF Mono. Apple devices use the system faces and download nothing.

Shape, space and elevation

Radii stay tight and step out concentrically: control 8, row and button 12, card 16, hero and sheet 20, a pill only for the iOS dock. A 4-point grid. A card's edge is drawn, one device pixel on a retina screen, with continuous corners where the browser can draw them. Shadows are tight and appear only where something floats.

Materials: glass on the controls, never on content

Liquid Glass carries the iPhone tab dock and its bottom accessory, the iPad sidebar, toolbars and sheets, and the system draws it. Content (the hero, cards, lists, charts, tiles) is never glass, glass never sits on glass, and the web PWA uses solid bars. At 20% brightness glass has to be dense, so its fill is 97% (light) and 94% (dark). These boards paint the layer flat, with no blur, because a dense fill over moving content only ghosts the words beneath it. Reduce Transparency makes it opaque on the device.

Icons

One sprite of 117 drawn icons on the web, SF Symbols in the Apple apps; the mapping names each symbol. Square caps, mitred joins and whole-pixel sizes (16, 20, 24, 28), with the stroke compensated at each size so all four read one weight. An icon beside a word is hidden from screen readers; an icon alone carries a label.

Claude's mark

Three prism facets in --mp-ai, a colour no figure uses, so words from Claude are never mistaken for numbers from a model. Claude's own logo is never drawn, and there is no sparkle. The mark moves only while Claude works; the Claude section shows how.

Components

Each module is composed the same way everywhere, and its states are drawn, not described. They follow the theme in the bar above.

Buttons

No buy, sell or order control exists anywhere. The strongest action in MP is recording a decision in your own journal. 50 pt primary, 44 pt small. A primary button has three authored fills: rest, hover a step brighter, pressed a step deeper. A press answers in the same frame and scales to 0.97.

Segmented control and the horizon switch

The selected segment is a strong fill (white words on ink in light, ink words on near white in dark) so it is plain at 20%; its lens slides by transform. The 3, 6 and 12-month horizons lead; 1 to 20 sessions sit apart, labelled short-term.

Short-term

Short-term forecasts carry their own amber label and their own calibration, and are never shown as investing guidance. On iPhone the horizon switch lives in the bottom accessory, in thumb reach.

Chips: status, freshness and provider

Every chip pairs a mark or an icon with a word, so it survives grayscale, colour blindness and 20% brightness.

The hero card

One per screen: midnight in light, lifted violet in dark. On the portfolio it carries the prism bar, your money split by segment, each cut piece in its field's brightest facet. On an instrument it takes that instrument's field and cut, and carries the forecast fan.

Holding row

Coin, name, venue, value and today's change on the first line; the 6-month range, its median and the ranked action on the second. Every range in a list shares one scale (−20% to +36%), so rows compare at a glance. The coin wears its segment's field, cut at one edge.

Segment tiles and the top-5 card

Tiles are the colourful way in; the list inside is always on a solid card. A score shows only when growth, protection and opportunity are all eligible, and a missing fifth place is said, not hidden.

Default · selected · forecast unavailable

Forecast card and chart

A forecast is never a bare number. History is a slate line; the live forecast is a violet median over its 50% and 80% bands; an evaluated past forecast is a dashed outline with its teal outcome. Scrub the chart with a pointer or the arrow keys: a lollipop reads out each month.

Ranked action

Add, hold, pause, trim and rebalance are decision support: each comes with its reason and its uncertainty, in neutral ink, never as an instruction.

Sheet and toast

Every confirmation is a sheet. It records a decision in your journal and never moves money; its figures sit on a solid card inside the glass.

Navigation: tab dock, sidebar, web bars, command bar

iPhone: a floating glass tab dock with a search orb, and a bottom accessory for the horizon. iPad: a floating glass sidebar. Web: solid bars, a 248 pt sidebar from 1024 px with a ⌘K command bar, a bottom tab bar below. The selected item is the same strong lens everywhere.

Motion

MyPaisa's motion (MA-182), held to a smoothness budget: quick, settled iOS springs that move only transform and opacity. Every demo replays; each names its SwiftUI form.

Curves and durations

Smooth for content arriving, snappy (a small overshoot) for a control answering a touch, bouncy only for a success, the system curve for sheets. Fast 180, base 340, slow 520, draw 950, count 750 ms; siblings 45 ms apart, at most eight.

Arrivals, the chart, the figure and the lens

Each frame plays once as it scrolls into view, started by an IntersectionObserver, never a scroll handler.

    Four beats

    1. ReadingYour question lands, the mark's three facets light in turn, and each read-only step lands as its tick draws.
    2. WritingSentences fade in one at a time. Figures are bold and each carries its source number.
    3. CitingThe source chips land in turn. A figure with no source never appears.
    4. Handing backThe actions rise and the mark rests. Claude proposes, you decide, nothing moves again.

    Four states that stand still

    • StoppedNothing was changed and no answer was saved.
    • RefusedMP cannot trade, so Claude says so and offers the forecast and its proof.
    • No evidenceClaude will not guess. No source and no model means no answer.
    • Claude offForecasts, ranges and scores keep working. Numbers never depend on Claude.

    The compact form sits in a row: .cl-chip.is-working says "Claude is reading 2 filings" on the iPhone home screen.

    The UX system

    One system for both hybrids: how a forecast is read, how navigation changes with width, the patterns, every state, and where the thumb reaches.

    Reading a forecast in three depths

    The same three depths on every surface: a row, a card, a sheet, a widget. Each depth is one tap from the next, and none is ever skipped.

    Navigation by width, not device

    The layout reads its own width. An iPhone is always compact; an iPad in Split View can be compact or regular; a laptop window can be any of the three.

    Patterns

    Six patterns recur on every screen. Each is drawn once here and used the same way everywhere.

    States, and each at 20%

    Freshness and the provider stay on screen in every state, the skeleton included. Under each state is the same part through the 20% filter, so you can see it hold.

    One-handed reach

    On iPhone the horizon switch and the primary actions sit in the lower half: the horizon in the bottom accessory above the tab dock, a decision's Record button at the foot of its sheet. Search and the profile, used less, may sit at the top.

    Screens

    Five screens at true size: nothing is scaled, so every word is as large as on the device. They follow the theme in the bar: switch to Dark to see each one at night. The wide boards scroll sideways inside their frame on a narrow window, or fit to its width.

    iPhone · home

    Holdings first, under the hero. Scroll on, and the segment fields follow as a carousel with the top 5 of the selected one. The horizon switch rides in the bottom accessory, above the glass tab dock.

    iPhone · instrument detail

    Apple at 6 months. The hero takes US tech's field and cut, and carries the fan; the full chart, the proof and the ranked action follow, each one tap from the next.

    iPad · split view

    Glass sidebar, the holdings list and the selected instrument's forecast side by side, at regular-to-wide width.

    Desktop · web dashboard

    Solid chrome, a 248 pt sidebar, the ⌘K command bar and 1.3 : 1 columns: holdings with shared-scale ranges, the selected forecast, its proof, the fields and provider health.

    PWA · 390 px

    The web app at phone width: a solid top bar, the bottom tab bar, denser rows and keyboard focus drawn.

    Contrast at full and 20% brightness

    Every pair that carries a word, a mark or a shape, measured in both themes from this page's own CSS, at full brightness (WCAG 2.2) and at 20%: (0.2·L1 + 0.05) / (0.2·L2 + 0.05), the panel's light at a fifth and the room's reflection kept. Body 7 and 4.5, text 7 and 3.5, marks 3 and 1.8, presence 1.5 and 1.25. A ground written a + b is a over b. Inset separators and chart grid lines are quiet on purpose and sit outside the tiers.
