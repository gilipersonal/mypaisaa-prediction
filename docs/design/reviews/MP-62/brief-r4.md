## MA's fonts and ink, as measured on the Home board

- **Figures:** `ui-rounded, "SF Pro Rounded", "Nunito"`, weight 800, tabular, tracking -0.03em.
- **Everything else:** `-apple-system, BlinkMacSystemFont, "SF Pro Text", "Inter"`. Large title 700 34/41 -0.02em; card
  title 600 20/25; row title and figure 600 15/20 -0.01em; secondary 400 13/18; caption 400 12/16; tab label 600 12/14;
  status bar 600 17/22; body 400 17/22. Weights are 400, 500, 600, 700 and, for figures, 800.
- **Ink:** two inks only, as the board uses: `#14171F` and `#4E5462` in light, `#F3F5F8` and `#C5CCD7` in dark. Round 1's
  third ink is retired. MA's coral is not ink and stays MA's: links keep Prism's own blue.

Because the owner chose this ink, the text tier is the 20% rule as first set, not round 3's raised one: body 7:1 and
4.5:1 at 20%; text 4.5:1 and 3:1. To let MA's ink hold it on Prism's grounds, the light ground rises a touch to
`#F4F6FB`, the dark card deepens a touch to `#0E142A`, and glass is denser (72%). Labels on glass use the primary ink.

A change happens in `tokens.json`, and the web, the PWA, iOS and iPadOS all follow it.

## Icons: cut from one facet

Drawn on a 24-point grid with a 2-point stroke, square ends and mitred corners. Each icon carries one flat cut facet, a
second tone across part of its shape: the idea behind SF Symbols' hierarchical rendering, made Prism's own. The facet
takes `--ic2` (default the icon's own colour at 26%). A selected tab fills the facet with colour, the way a selected tab
fills on iOS. In the Apple app, SF Symbols carry the same two rules with `.symbolRenderingMode(.hierarchical)` and
`.palette`.

## Motion: subtle, everywhere

MA's curves and durations (they are tokens). Each frame plays once as it scrolls into view: cards rise, rows follow on a
45 ms stagger capped at eight, chips settle, charts draw in, then dots and labels land, bars grow, figures roll a short
way to their value and keep their width. Every control answers the finger: press scale, a sliding lens on segmented
controls and the tab bar, tile and row lift on hover. Sheets rise on the system curve. Skeletons and spinners stop on
their own. Only transform and opacity animate (clip-path and stroke-dashoffset for a draw-in), nothing loops forever,
Reduce Motion lands at once. Subtle means under a third of a second for anything the reader waits on, and no moment that
asks to be looked at.

## Structure

A sticky section index on the left from 1024 px, a scrolling chip row below that, the current section lit, in exactly
this order: **Principles, Three depths, Foundations, Components, Patterns, States, Layout, Screens, Motion, Claude,
Contrast at 20%, Web and SwiftUI.**

## What makes it robust

- **Foundations:** three tiers of token (primitive values, semantic roles, component use), named once, with how a change
  flows from `tokens.json` to both platforms.
- **Components:** each with its anatomy, every state, its sizes, its do and don't, its accessibility note, its status
  label, and its web and SwiftUI names.
- **Patterns and States:** one way to do each thing, the same on every surface.
- **Layout:** width classes, not devices: compact under 600, regular 600 to 1023, wide from 1024, with container queries.
  Desktop multi-pane; iPhone one-handed reach; iPad split view, Stage Manager and rotation; PWA install, standalone
  display, offline, safe-area insets and no hover-only control.
- **Web and SwiftUI:** the token and component contract; Dynamic Type on every style; SF Symbols; `.glassEffect()` on the
  control layer; size classes; haptics. Then how the system is run: semantic versions, how a change is proposed and
  approved, deprecation, and the status labels.

## Rules that do not change

Round 1's product rules and shared figures hold: MP never trades, no personalised advice, a forecast is never a bare
number, freshness always in view, every figure illustrative, holdings first, nothing copied from Monzo, Klarna, Apple or
MA's brand.
