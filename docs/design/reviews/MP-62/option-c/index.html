Ink is fact. Ultramarine is forecast.

Signal is high-contrast monochrome with one electric colour, and that colour has one job: it marks what a model expects. History, holdings and controls stay in ink, so a forecast can never pass for a fact.

  • InkWhat happened: prices, values, history
  • Signal UltramarineWhat the model expects: ranges, medians
  • Up and downOnly with a sign and an arrow, never colour alone

Illustrative Every figure on this page is an example from the MP-62 brief, not a real holding. MyPrediction cannot trade and gives no personal advice. Not approved UX; nothing is built.

Why ultramarine

It can't be mistaken for gain or loss: it sits far from both red and green, including for red-green colour blindness. It reads as text on white (7.0:1) and, lifted, on true black (7.1:1). It is not lime, not coral, and not the saffron or prism colours of options A and B.

What comes from Monzo

The website's poster scale: oversized figures, pence set smaller, black display slabs on white, round instrument discs and modules that compose the same way on every screen. No Monzo colour, typeface or copy.

What comes from Apple

Large titles, a tab bar on iPhone and a sidebar on iPad and desktop, sheets for confirmation, 44-point targets, semantic colours, and materials only in bars and sheets, never under a figure.

Foundations

Semantic tokens, measured on the background they actually sit on. Light is crisp white; dark is true black for OLED, with every value stepped by hand rather than inverted.

Red and green fail colour-blind separation on their own (deuteranopia), which is why rise and fall always carry a sign and a triangle. The accent never sits next to them as a data series.

Three kinds of figure, three looks

History, evaluated forecasts and conditional scenarios never look alike. A scenario claims no likelihood, so it gets no fill and no colour.

History

Solid ink line, 2 px. Prices that happened, with their provider and timestamp.

Forecast

Ultramarine fan: 80% range edged, 50% range filled, median line. Never a bare number.

Scored outcome

The old range as a bracket. A filled dot landed inside; a ring missed. Misses are shown, not hidden.

Scenario · 3 to 5 years

Dashed, unfilled, grey ink. Says "if this, then that". It is never given a likelihood.

Icons

One authored set on a 24-point grid, 1.75 stroke, round caps; 2.25 when selected. Every icon that carries meaning sits beside a word. In SwiftUI, SF Symbols stand in where a close match exists.

Motion

Quick and exponential: things arrive fast and settle. Figures update in place with a short cross-fade, never a slot-machine roll that would tempt a second look at a price. With Reduce Motion on, every movement becomes a 120 ms cross-fade or the final state.

Screens

Each screen in light and dark, built from the components above with the brief's shared figures. Phone screens scroll inside the device.

iPhone · Home

Holdings first, under the portfolio figure; then the top 5 per segment.

What to notice

  • The figure is the hero, on a black slab with the providers and cutoff underneath it.
  • Every row puts its 6-month range on the same scale, so NVIDIA's width is obvious next to the ETF's.
  • HSBC is stale: muted value, amber clock, Pause with its reason.
  • Gold says "forecast unavailable" instead of showing an empty card.

iPhone · Instrument detail

A forecast for a chosen horizon, with its proof one scroll away: calibration, track record, model version.

What to notice

  • The median is big but never alone: both ranges, the horizon, as-of, cutoff and model sit under it.
  • The May 2026 forecast is scored on the chart itself.
  • Scenarios are dashed and unfilled, and claim no likelihood.
  • Decision support says why, and that MyPrediction cannot trade.

iPad · Split view

Sidebar, holdings list and detail. The selected row takes the accent wash because it is showing a forecast.

Desktop · Dashboard

The editorial layout: a display slab for the figure, a dense holdings table at trading density, and the segment lists beside it.

PWA · 390 px

The web door at phone width: the same modules, a web header with search, and a bottom bar that follows the app's tabs.

What to notice

  • Same tokens and components as iOS, so web and Apple build from one system.
