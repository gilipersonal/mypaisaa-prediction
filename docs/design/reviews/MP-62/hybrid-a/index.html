Six principles, each ruling something out

Hybrid A and Hybrid B share all six, with the same type, shapes, components, states and motion. Only colour and the signature motif differ.

Cold daylight, violet ink, cut edges

MyPaisa's fonts and motion on a colder, sharper ground, carrying MP's own colour: near-black ink and midnight on white by day, pure white on violet ink by night, saffron and gold for forecasts, teal and mint for scored outcomes. Every neutral is cold, so the identity colours stand alone on the screen. No MyPaisa coral, copy or figures.

Colour

Each token has a light and a dark value (left and right half of each swatch). What 7:1 and 3.5:1 at 20% force: a ground that carries text is near white by day and near black by night; a filled button is near black with a white label by day and near white with an ink label by night; coloured text is deep by day and pastel by night. Saffron cannot carry text, so it fills bands and marks, and forecast text is deep burnt orange by day and gold by night.

Type

Figures use SF Pro Rounded at 800 (Nunito on the web), tabular, tracked −0.03 em: big, round and steady while they roll. The interface uses SF Pro Text (Inter on the web) on Apple's Dynamic Type Large scale. Model ids and hashes use SF Mono. Hierarchy comes from size and weight, never from dim grey. Nothing is under 12 px except tab bar labels and chart ticks at 11.5 px; nothing is lighter than 400.

Figures

Pounds big, pence at 55%. Columns line up on tabular digits. A fall uses a true minus and a falling mark in quiet ink; a rise uses + and a rising mark in green; colour is never the only sign.

Shape, space and depth

Concentric radii from the control out: 8, 12, 16, 20 and the pill, on Apple's continuous corners where the browser can draw them. A 4 px grid. Every target 44 px; primary buttons 50 px. Every edge is drawn: a hairline, one device pixel on a retina screen. Depth is a tight offset shadow, and the larger two only where something floats. The one midnight hero card per screen carries the portfolio.

Materials

Liquid Glass belongs to the iOS and iPadOS control layer only: the tab dock, the sidebar, toolbars. Never on content, never glass on glass, never on the web PWA, which uses solid bars. To stay legible at 20%, MP's glass is dense (97% by day, 94% by night) with a drawn hairline edge. The apps use the system material at that density; this page paints it flat, with no blur.

Icons

One inline SVG sprite of icons: Lucide's geometry redrawn with square caps and mitred joins, plus MP's own fan glyphs. SF Symbols stay the native mapping. Icons sit on whole pixels at 16, 20, 24 and 28 px, with the stroke compensated so every size reads the same weight. An icon beside a word is hidden from screen readers; an icon-only control carries a name.

Every part, in every state

The controls here work: tap the segments, the horizon switch and the tabs; scrub the chart with a pointer or the arrow keys. Nothing here buys, sells or places an order. The strongest action is recording your own decision.

Buttons

One primary per screen: midnight with a white label by day, near white with an ink label by night. Rest, hover and pressed are three distinct fills, and pressing scales to 0.97 in the same frame as the touch. Disabled is hatched, so its words stay legible without being faded. A loading button spins at most six turns, then says what is still happening.

Segmented control and the fan horizon switch

The selected segment is a solid lens, midnight by day and near white by night, the only selection that still reads at 20%. It slides to the tapped item by transform. The horizon switch is a fan: each long-term horizon's wedge opens wider, and 5 and 20 sessions sit apart, labelled short-term. At least 8 px inside every segment.

Chips, switches and meters

A chip pairs its colour with a word and, where it helps, an icon. Saffron tints mean forecast, teal a scored outcome, violet a scenario.

Holding row and Top 5 card

A grouped row with a round monogram: name and value, ticker and today, then the forecast as a fan glyph on one shared scale (−30% to +30%) so rows compare at a glance. The Top 5 score shows only when growth, protection and opportunity are all eligible; an empty rank says why.

Forecast range, three densities

Full with labelled values for the detail; a ribbon for tables; the fan glyph for rows. A forecast never appears as a bare number. The 50% band is drawn from the same illustrative distribution as the 80% range.

Price chart: history, the live fan and a scored forecast

History is a solid ink line. The live forecast is the fan, hard wedges with a dashed median. A past forecast is a dashed outline with its outcome dot where the price landed. Scenarios are violet dots and claim no likelihood. Scrub with a pointer, or focus the chart and use the arrow keys.

Ranked actions, sheet and toast

Ranked actions are decision support: each has its own shape, so the five read apart without colour, and each comes with its reasons and its range. Pause is the only tinted one: it says the data is not fit to rank. Every confirmation is a sheet; a toast confirms and offers undo.

Navigation, search and the command bar

On the web the bars are solid: a tab bar under 1024 px, a sidebar from 1024 px, and ⌘K from anywhere. On iPhone the tab dock floats in dense glass; on iPad the sidebar does. Tap the tabs: the lens slides.

Every state is drawn, and drawn again at 20%

Freshness and the provider stay on screen in every state, also while loading. Each tile shows the state at full brightness and, beside it, through the same 20% filter as the page bar's preview.

Navigation by width, not device

The app asks how wide its window is, never what device it runs on: a phone, an iPad in Split View and a narrow browser window all get the compact layout. The iPhone tab dock and the iPad sidebar are Liquid Glass; the web PWA uses solid bars at every width.

One-handed reach

On iPhone, everything you change sits in the lower half, where a thumb reaches without a grip change: the horizon switch rides in a glass accessory above the tab dock, the primary button sits at the foot of its card, and sheets rise from the bottom with their buttons last.

The top third is for reading: the large title, the hero figure, freshness. Nothing in it needs a tap to understand the screen.

On iPad and desktop the same controls sit beside what they change, at the top of the detail column.

Quick, settled, never showy

MyPaisa's motion system (MA-182), moved onto MP's smoothness budget: content moves, the bars stay put; only transform and opacity animate; nothing repeats more than six times; under Reduce Motion an arrival lands at once and a change of state is a 150 ms cross-fade.

Curves and durations

Smooth for content arriving, snappy (a small overshoot) for a control answering a touch, bouncy only for success, the system curve for sheets. Fast 180, base 340, slow 520, draw 950 and count 750 ms; siblings 45 ms apart, at most eight.

Arrivals, the fan and the figure roll

Each plays as its frame scrolls into view, started by an IntersectionObserver, never a scroll handler. The chart draws in left to right, then the fan opens from today, then its dots and labels land. A figure rolls to its value once, keeping its width.

Claude reads, writes, cites, then hands back

Numbers come from code and models; words come from Claude. The motion says which is which: the mark moves only while Claude is working, every figure it writes carries its source, and nothing moves once the decision is yours.

Four beats

  • Reading

    Your question lands. The mark's three blades light in turn, and each read-only step lands as its tick draws.

  • Writing

    Sentences fade in one at a time. Figures are bold and each carries its source number.

  • Citing

    The source chips land in turn. A figure with no source never appears.

  • Handing back

    The actions rise and the mark rests. Claude proposes, you decide, and nothing moves again.

Four states that stand still

  • Stopped

    You stopped it. Nothing changed and no answer was saved.

  • Refused

    MyPrediction cannot trade, so Claude says so and offers the forecast instead.

  • No evidence

    No model or source covers the question, so Claude will not guess.

  • Claude off

    Numbers never depend on Claude: forecasts, ranges and scores all still work.

The mark, and its compact form

Three fan blades cut from the same wedge as the forecast, in --mp-ai. Each blade lights in turn, at most six times, then the mark rests in words. Claude's own logo is never drawn, and there is no sparkle. In a row, the compact chip says what is happening: "Claude is reading 2 filings" on the iPhone home screen.
