The UX system · shared by both hybrids

Six principles

Each one rules something out. Hybrid A and Hybrid B share all of them, with the same type, shapes, components, states and motion; only colour and the signature motif differ.

Three depths, on every surface

Foundations

Warm ledger, held to 20%

MyPaisa's type, shapes, surfaces, elevation, glass and motion, carrying MP's own colour: ink and navy on paper, saffron for forecasts, teal and mint for scored outcomes. No MyPaisa coral, copy or figures.

Colour

Each token has a light and a dark value (left and right half of each swatch). Bands and glass are shown over a card. What 20% brightness forces: a ground that carries body text is near white by day and near black by night; a filled button is near black with a white label by day and near white with an ink label by night; saffron cannot carry text, so it fills bands and marks, and forecast text is dark amber by day and gold by night.

Type

Figures use SF Pro Rounded at 800 (Nunito on the web), tabular, tracked −0.03 em: big, round and steady while they roll. The interface uses SF Pro Text (Inter on the web) on Apple's Dynamic Type Large scale. Model ids and hashes use SF Mono. Nothing is under 12 px except tab bar labels and chart ticks at 11 px; nothing is lighter than 400.

Figures

Pounds big, pence at 55%. Columns line up on tabular digits. A fall uses a true minus and ▼ in quiet ink; a rise uses + and ▲ in green; colour is never the only sign.

Shape, space and depth

Concentric radii from the control out: 10, 14, 16, 20, 24 and the pill, so an inner corner always sits parallel to its outer one. A 4 px grid. Every target 44 px; primary buttons 50 px. Cards lift with a soft two-layer shadow; grouped lists stay flat with inset hairlines; the one navy hero card per screen carries the portfolio.

Materials

Liquid Glass belongs to the iOS and iPadOS control layer only: the tab dock, the sidebar, toolbars. Never on content, never glass on glass, never on the web PWA, which uses solid paper bars. To stay legible at 20%, MP's glass is dense (96% by day, 97% by night) with a 16 px blur; Reduce Transparency makes it solid.

Icons

Material Symbols Rounded at weight 500 on the web, SF Symbols in the apps; the filled form marks the selected tab. An icon beside a word is hidden from screen readers; an icon-only control carries a name.

Components

Every part, in every state

The controls here work: tap the segments, the horizon switch and the tabs; scrub the chart with a pointer or the arrow keys. Nothing here buys, sells or places an order. The strongest action is recording your own decision.

Buttons

One primary per screen: ink navy with a white label by day, paper with an ink label by night. Pressed scales to 0.97 in the same frame as the touch. Disabled keeps its words at 3:1 without fading them. A loading button spins at most six turns, then says what is still happening.

Segmented control and the fan horizon switch

The selected segment is a solid navy lens (paper at night), the only selection that still reads at 20%. It slides to the tapped item by transform. The horizon switch is a fan: each long-term horizon's wedge opens wider, and 5 and 20 sessions sit apart, labelled short-term. At least 8 px inside every segment.

Chips, switches and meters

A chip pairs its colour with a word and, where it helps, an icon. Saffron chips mean forecast, teal chips a scored outcome, violet a scenario.

Holding row and Top 5 card

A grouped row with a round monogram: name and value, ticker and today, then the forecast as a fan glyph on one shared scale (−30% to +30%) so rows compare at a glance. The Top 5 score shows only when growth, protection and opportunity are all eligible; an empty rank says why.

Forecast range, three densities

Full with labelled values for the detail; a ribbon for tables; the fan glyph for rows. A forecast never appears as a bare number. The 50% band is drawn from the same illustrative distribution as the 80% range.

Price chart: history, the live fan and a scored forecast

History is a solid ink line. The live forecast is the saffron fan with a dashed median. A past forecast is a dashed outline with its outcome dot where the price landed. Scenarios are violet dots and claim no likelihood. Scrub with a pointer, or focus the chart and use the arrow keys.

Ranked actions, sheet and toast

Ranked actions are decision support: each has its own shape, so the five read apart without colour, and each comes with its reasons and its range. Pause is the only tinted one: it says the data is not fit to rank. Every confirmation is a sheet; a toast confirms and offers undo.

Navigation, search and the command bar

On the web the bars are solid paper: a tab bar under 1024 px, a sidebar from 1024 px, and ⌘K from anywhere. On iPhone the tab dock floats in glass; on iPad the sidebar does. Tap the tabs: the lens slides.

Patterns

How the parts behave together

Six patterns a builder follows the same way on every surface.

States

Every state is drawn, and drawn again at 20%

Freshness and the provider stay on screen in every state, also while loading. Each tile shows the state at full brightness and, beside it, through the same 20% filter as the page bar's preview.

Layout

Navigation by width, not device

The app asks how wide its window is, never what device it runs on: a phone, an iPad in Split View and a narrow browser window all get the compact layout. The iPhone tab dock and the iPad sidebar are Liquid Glass; the web PWA uses solid paper bars at every width.

One-handed reach

On iPhone, everything you change sits in the lower half, where a thumb reaches without a grip change: the horizon switch rides in a glass accessory above the tab dock, the primary button sits at the foot of its card, and sheets rise from the bottom with their buttons last.

The top third is for reading: the large title, the hero figure, freshness. Nothing in it needs a tap to understand the screen.

On iPad and desktop the same controls sit beside what they change, at the top of the detail column.

Screens

Five screens, one system

Drawn in the page's theme from the same tokens: switch the page between light and dark to compare. Phones scroll inside their frame; the iPad and the desktop keep their own widths and scroll sideways inside their board on a narrow window. The figures are the shared illustrative set.

Motion

Quick, settled, never showy

MyPaisa's motion system (MA-182), moved onto MP's smoothness budget: content moves, the bars stay put; only transform and opacity animate; nothing repeats more than six times; under Reduce Motion an arrival lands at once and a change of state is a 150 ms cross-fade.

Curves and durations

Smooth for content arriving, snappy (a small overshoot) for a control answering a touch, bouncy only for success, the system curve for sheets. Fast 180, base 340, slow 520, draw 950 and count 750 ms; siblings 45 ms apart, at most eight.

Arrivals, the fan and the figure roll

Each plays as its frame scrolls into view, started by an IntersectionObserver, never a scroll handler. The chart draws in left to right, then the fan opens from today, then its dots and labels land. A figure rolls to its value once, keeping its width.

Contrast at full and 20% brightness

Every pair, measured from this page's own tokens

For two colours of relative luminance L1 > L2: full brightness is (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05), WCAG 2.2; at 20% it is (0.2·L1 + 0.05) / (0.2·L2 + 0.05), because the screen's light falls to a fifth while the room's reflection stays. A ground written a + b is a composited over b. The table reads the token values from this page's style sheet when it loads, so it cannot drift from what is drawn.
